⚠️ Careful: “have an attack”

“I had an attack when I saw him” sounds incomplete or even medical (a panic attack? an asthma attack?). It’s not how people usually describe excitement. Say instead:

✅ I freaked out.   ✅ I lost my mind.   ✅ I had a fit (very informal, funny and exaggerated; it can also mean “got angry”, so use context).

In context

Mia: Okay, so I literally saw Jackson Wang at the airport.

Kai: Stop. Did you freak out?

Imagine this… with WOULD

The idea: use would for things that aren’t real, just imagined.
If + past → would + verb: If I met him, I would probably freak out.

Pro tip: answer with I’d…, then add “because…” and a follow-up thought. B2 speakers always explain!

📖 Word focus: to grope

grope (verb) = to touch someone’s body, especially private parts, in a sexual way without their permission. It is a form of sexual harassment or assault, so it’s serious. It is never “just excitement”, and it can happen to anyone, including famous men.

Another meaning: grope for something = to search for it with your hands because you can’t see well: I groped for the light switch.

Show the conjugation

Regular verb. Because it already ends in -e, you add only -d for the past, and you drop the e before -ing.
